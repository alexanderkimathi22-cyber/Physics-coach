Physics lessons

1. Motion

Distance is the total path travelled. Displacement is the change in position in a direction. Speed is distance per time; velocity is displacement per time.

Speed = distance ÷ time

Acceleration, a = (v − u) ÷ t

For uniform acceleration:

v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²

Symbols: u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement.

2. Forces

A force can change an object's speed, direction or shape. The SI unit is the newton (N). Balanced forces give zero resultant force.

Resultant force, F = ma
Weight, W = mg

Mass is measured in kilograms; weight is a force measured in newtons. Friction opposes relative motion.

3. Work, energy and power

Work is done when a force moves an object through a distance in the force's direction. Energy is the capacity to do work.

Work = force × distance
Kinetic energy = ½mv²
Gravitational P.E. = mgh
Power = work ÷ time

Energy changes form, but total energy is conserved in a closed system.

4. Electricity

Electric current is the rate of flow of charge. Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge. Resistance opposes current.

Current, I = Q ÷ t
V = IR
Electrical power, P = VI

In a series circuit, current is the same through each component. In a parallel circuit, potential difference is the same across each branch.

5. Waves

A wave transfers energy without overall transfer of matter. Transverse waves vibrate perpendicular to travel; longitudinal waves vibrate parallel to travel.

Wave speed, v = fλ

Frequency f is measured in hertz (Hz), wavelength λ in metres (m), and wave speed in metres per second (m/s). Reflection is bouncing back; refraction is a change in direction due to a change in speed.

6. Interference

Interference occurs when waves overlap. Constructive interference produces a larger amplitude; destructive interference reduces the resultant amplitude.

Bright fringe: path difference = nλ
Dark fringe: path difference = (n + ½)λ

Here n = 0, 1, 2, … for bright fringes, and λ is the wavelength.

Young’s double-slit experiment

Light from two narrow, closely spaced slits spreads out and overlaps on a screen. The overlapping waves form alternating bright and dark fringes. A stable pattern is observed when the slits are illuminated by coherent light.

Fringe-spacing formula

β = λD / a
  • β = distance between adjacent bright fringes (m)
  • λ = wavelength of light (m)
  • D = distance from the slits to the screen (m)
  • a = separation of the two slits (m)

Example: If λ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁷ m, D = 2.0 m and a = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m:

β = (6.0 × 10⁻⁷ × 2.0) ÷ (3.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 4.0 × 10⁻³ m, or 4.0 mm.

KCSE-style practice

Try each question before tapping Show answer. These are revision questions written for practice, not official examination questions.

1. A car travels 150 m in 10 s. Calculate its average speed.

Speed = distance ÷ time = 150 ÷ 10 = 15 m/s.

2. A resultant force of 12 N acts on a 3 kg object. Find its acceleration.

Using F = ma, a = F ÷ m = 12 ÷ 3 = 4 m/s².

3. A 2 kg object is lifted vertically through 5 m. Take g = 10 N/kg. Find the gain in gravitational potential energy.

P.E. = mgh = 2 × 10 × 5 = 100 J.

4. A resistor of 4 Ω is connected across 12 V. Calculate the current.

Using V = IR, I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 A.

5. A wave has frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 0.4 m. Find its speed.

v = fλ = 50 × 0.4 = 20 m/s.

6. In Young’s experiment, λ = 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m, D = 1.5 m and a = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ m. Calculate β.

β = λD/a = (5.0 × 10⁻⁷ × 1.5) ÷ (2.5 × 10⁻⁴) = 3.0 × 10⁻³ m = 3.0 mm.

7. State one difference between transverse and longitudinal waves.

In transverse waves, vibrations are perpendicular to the direction of travel. In longitudinal waves, vibrations are parallel to the direction of travel.

8. What is the difference between series and parallel circuits?

A series circuit has one path for current. A parallel circuit has separate branches, giving more than one path for current.

Study advice for KCSE

  • Study a small topic daily instead of cramming everything at once.
  • Write down each formula, define every symbol and include SI units.
  • Practise rearranging formulas before substituting numbers.
  • Draw clear, labelled diagrams for experiments and circuits.
  • Show your working in calculations and check units in the final answer.
  • After marking your practice, redo every question you missed without looking at the solution.
  • Use your teacher’s notes and the current KCSE syllabus to guide complete revision.
